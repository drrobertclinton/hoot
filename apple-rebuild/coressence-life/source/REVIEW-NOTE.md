# App Review note: Coressence Life 1.2.0 (build 4)

This version answers the 4.3(a) rejection of 1.1.0. The app was redesigned around what only it does.

Coressence Life is the companion to two courses by Robert Clinton, MD (Live at Your Peak, Perform at Your Peak). Their text, worksheets and homework are in the app, and the method they teach (assess, plan, do, measure, review, adjust, keep what works) is now its structure:

- Home is the weekly Peak Cycle: a six-step loop, this week's module, today's one to three actions and the biggest signal in the user's data. The ring dashboard and five-tab bar are replaced by this screen and a small floating dock (This week, Course, Toolkit).
- Weekly Experiment: one change from the course, measured before and after from the user's own data, with a keep, adjust or drop verdict.
- Performer Day for singers, actors and speakers: show countdown, voice-load budget, warmups and performance-day sequence.
- Why this number: the readiness score taken apart, point by point.
- All of Coressence (grid icon on Home, and at the top of Toolkit): one page that opens every section of the program: Move, Fuel, Sleep, Recover, Restore, Rehearse, Track, Measure, Monitor, Devices, Labs, Stack Check, Courses, Resources, Plan, Coach, MD and Shop.
- Connected devices has a step-by-step guide (Devices, then "Connect my device, step by step") that explains how to turn on Apple Health sharing in each brand's own app (watches, rings, glucose sensors, heart rate straps, blood pressure cuffs, scales). The app itself only reads Apple Health, with the user's permission; data stays on the phone.
- Monitor, then Glucose, shows readings that a glucose sensor's own app saved to Apple Health, beside the meals logged in Fuel and the workouts recorded on a watch (the two hours after a meal; before and after a workout). The user can mark "I'm eating now" to keep the pre-meal and two-hour readings side by side, and the app lists past rises with no meal marked so the user can confirm or dismiss them. When past readings in the last 24 hours were under 70, under 54 or over 250 mg/dL, the screen shows a review notice that says to keep alerts on in the sensor's own app and to talk with a clinician. It is display only: no push or real-time alerts, no dosing, no diagnosis, and the screen says to use the sensor's own app for treatment decisions.
- Stack Check is a personal list of the medicines and supplements a user takes, arranged by time of day, with a text summary they can share with their pharmacist or clinician. It does not check interactions, calculate or suggest doses, or give medical advice, and says so on screen.
- Resources and books lists the two course books (their full text is in the app), the worksheets, the in-app guides and a reading list of the author's six forthcoming books, each linked to the matching section of the app. It has no outbound links and nothing for sale.
- Coressence MD is an information page for a separate telehealth service from the author's medical practice, available today only to people located in North Carolina. The app takes no payment for it and collects no medical information; "Request a visit" opens the user's email app.
- Coressence Shop is a gear guide (what to look for). There is nothing to buy in the app and it has no outbound links.

Our other apps are unrelated in subject, content and code: a watch collection catalog, a wine cellar inventory, a gift planner, a purchase-reflection ledger and a children's activity app. None contain course material, health data or performer tools.

Health data is read-only and stays on the device.
